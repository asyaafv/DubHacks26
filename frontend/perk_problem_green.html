Good to know

Frequently asked questions

A few answers about how PERK is designed to make rewards easier to understand and use.

What makes PERK innovative compared with existing credit card rewards apps?

PERK is designed to bring cards, memberships, and offer details into one experience, then explain useful combinations in a clear, practical way. Rather than simply listing rewards, the goal is to help users understand which options fit a purchase and why.

How many steps does it take for a new user to start receiving useful recommendations?

The intended experience is to get started by adding the relevant cards, memberships, or offer information, then view recommendations based on what is available. The exact number of steps depends on the final onboarding flow and how much information a user chooses to add; this prototype does not yet establish a fixed step count.

Why did you choose a website and Chrome extension?

A website gives users a place to organize their rewards and explore recommendations, while a Chrome extension can bring relevant guidance closer to the moment they are browsing or shopping online. Together, they are intended to make rewards easier to use without adding friction to everyday routines.

How does PERK help users who don't know much about credit card rewards?

PERK aims to translate reward rules into straightforward guidance, explain why an option may be useful, and surface relevant benefits without requiring users to study every program's terms themselves. Users should still be able to review the underlying terms before making a purchase.

How does PERK know whether two offers can actually be stacked?

A reliable match needs to check the terms and eligibility rules for both offers, including restrictions, dates, merchants, and exclusions. Where the rules are incomplete or ambiguous, PERK should flag the combination as unconfirmed rather than promise that both offers will apply. The exact verification method depends on the final product implementation.
